01 · Body relationship

Shift from inspection to relationship.

Body distress often gets louder when appearance becomes the main way you evaluate yourself. These exercises broaden the lens: function, values, comfort, needs, and lived experience.

02 · Thought patterns

Separate the thought from the truth.

Rigid rules, perfectionism, and conditional self-worth can make body-image distress feel factual. This section helps you examine the rule rather than obey it automatically.

1. Catch itWrite the exact rule, prediction, or self-criticism your mind is repeating.
2. Test itAsk what evidence supports it, what evidence complicates it, and whose standards it reflects.
3. Replace itChoose a statement that is more accurate, humane, and workable.

Thought reframe

Conditional worth detector

Complete the sentence, then ask whether you are postponing permission to live, connect, dress, eat, rest, or seek help.

I keep telling myself I can finally ______ when ______.
Try this alternative: “I do not have to earn basic care, belonging, or dignity by changing my body.”

Compassion test

Imagine someone you deeply care about believed the same rule. What would you want them to know?

Control vs. influence

Body-image anxiety often narrows attention around control. Separate what you cannot control from what you can influence today.

03 · Media literacy

Pause before a message becomes a rule.

Online content is created by someone, for a reason, in a context. You can slow down the process and decide whether a message deserves space in your life.

SourceWho made this? What expertise, lived experience, incentives, or blind spots might shape it?
PurposeIs the content informing, entertaining, selling, persuading, comparing, or seeking engagement?
ImpactWhat happens in me after I see it? More grounded, curious, pressured, ashamed, activated, or compelled?

Analyze one post

Feed impact scan

Select what you commonly notice after scrolling.

Select any patterns that fit, then check your pattern.

Feed boundary options

Before I follow

Ask: Does this account support the kind of relationship I want with my body and mind?

Before I post

If you share vulnerable content, protect both yourself and the people who may encounter it. Avoid turning distress into a competition or giving triggering behavioral detail.

04 · Comparison

Turn comparison into information.

Comparison can point to a need, fear, value, or longing. It does not have to become a verdict about your worth.

Comparison decoder

Translate envy into a value

Sometimes the useful question is not “How do I become them?” but “What quality or experience am I longing for?”

Analytics detox

If likes, views, follower counts, body measurements, or other numbers start defining success, pick a non-numeric measure that reflects your values.

05 · Community

Build support without giving away your agency.

Connection can reduce isolation, but healthy support should not replace your own judgment or professional care when you need it.

My support map

How I want support to sound

Support is usually more helpful when it is curious, non-comparative, and not coercive.

One small act of connection

Choose something manageable: reply to a trusted person, schedule time together, or send a supportive message.

One boundary that protects me

Boundaries can be about topics, time, accounts, comments, DMs, or what you share publicly.

Professional support

If body-image distress, eating behaviors, compulsive exercise, anxiety, depression, or self-harm thoughts are interfering with daily life, consider involving a qualified health professional rather than trying to manage it entirely through self-help or social media.

06 · My plan

Turn insight into a small next step.

Keep the plan realistic. The goal is not perfect body confidence; it is a more respectful, flexible, and self-directed relationship with your body and media environment.

Before you begin

This tool is for reflection and psychoeducation. It is not a diagnosis or a substitute for medical, nutritional, or mental-health treatment.

If reflecting on food, weight, exercise, body image, or social media starts to intensify urges to harm yourself, restrict food, purge, binge, overexercise, or otherwise put your health at risk, stop the exercise and contact a qualified professional or trusted support person. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services in your area.
